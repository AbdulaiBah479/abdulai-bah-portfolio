# Glossary (plain language)

**API / API key**: a way for software to talk to a service; the key is its password. Give trading keys *no withdrawal* permission.
**Ask / Bid**: lowest price a seller accepts / highest a buyer offers. **Spread** = the gap = a hidden cost.
**ATR (Average True Range)**: typical daily price movement; used to set stops.
**Backtest**: replaying a rule on past data to see what it would have done.
**Benchmark**: the simple alternative you must beat (e.g. a global index ETF).
**Bond / Coupon / Maturity / YTM**: a loan to a government/company / its interest / the payback date / total yearly return if held to maturity.
**Broker**: company that lets you trade. **Exchange**: the marketplace (Binance, NYSE).
**Candlestick**: a bar showing open, high, low, close for a period.
**CAGR**: compound annual growth rate.
**CFD**: a leveraged bet on price without owning the asset. Most retail clients lose.
**Cooling-off lock**: this project's rule that blocks sizing trades after 2 losses in a row.
**DCA (dollar-cost averaging)**: investing a fixed amount on a schedule.
**Delta / Theta (options)**: price sensitivity / daily time decay.
**Dividend / Ex-dividend date / DRIP**: cash paid to shareholders / the cut-off to qualify / automatic reinvestment.
**Drawdown / Max drawdown**: fall from a peak to a low. A 50% drawdown needs a +100% gain to recover.
**EMA / SMA**: exponential / simple moving average (smoothed price line).
**ETF**: a fund that trades like a share and holds a basket of assets.
**Expectancy**: average result per trade (in R). Must be positive after costs.
**Fee / Slippage**: charged by the exchange / difference between expected and actual fill price.
**Forex (FX)**: currency trading. **Pip**: 0.0001 price move. **Lot**: trade size (100,000 units standard).
**Futures**: contract to buy/sell later, with leverage and margin.
**Gap**: price jumps past your stop (common in stocks overnight).
**Hedge**: a position that offsets another risk.
**Index fund**: fund copying a market index.
**Inflation**: rising prices that erode cash.
**Leverage / Margin / Liquidation**: trading borrowed money / the collateral / forced closing when losses eat the collateral.
**Limit order / Market order / Stop order**: price-controlled / immediate / triggers at a set price. **OCO**: take-profit + stop pair.
**Liquidity**: how easily you can trade without moving the price.
**Long / Short**: betting on a rise / a fall.
**Look-ahead bias**: accidentally using future data in a test (creates fake results).
**Maker / Taker**: adds an order to the book (cheaper fee) / takes an existing order.
**Momentum**: assets that have been rising tend to keep rising (for a while).
**Monte Carlo**: simulate thousands of random futures to see the range of outcomes.
**Out-of-sample**: data the strategy was never tuned on.
**Overfitting**: tuning a rule so tightly to history that it fails on new data.
**Paper trading**: practice with fake money. **Testnet**: an exchange's fake-money copy.
**Payout ratio**: share of profit paid as dividends.
**Position sizing**: deciding how much to buy based on risk, not feelings.
**Profit factor**: gross profit ÷ gross loss.
**R (R-multiple)**: your result measured in units of the amount you risked. +2R = won twice what you risked.
**Rebalance**: restoring target percentages of each asset.
**RSI**: momentum gauge 0–100; low = recent selling, high = recent buying.
**Risk of ruin**: chance of losing so much you can't continue.
**Sharpe / Sortino / Calmar**: return per unit of volatility / of downside volatility / per unit of max drawdown.
**Spot**: buying the real asset immediately (no leverage).
**Stablecoin**: crypto pegged to a currency (USDT, USDC); can lose its peg.
**Stop-loss / Take-profit**: automatic exit to cap loss / lock gain.
**Trend following**: buy rising, avoid falling assets; many small losses, a few big wins.
**Volatility**: how much price swings. **Vol-targeting**: smaller size when swings are large.
**Walk-forward**: testing on consecutive time windows.
**Yield curve**: bond yields across maturities; inverted curve often precedes recessions.
**Yield trap**: very high yield that exists because the price is collapsing.
